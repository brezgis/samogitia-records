# Lithuanian translation glossary: Plungė baptism registers, 1849–1907

This glossary covers all five Plungė books:

| Book | Years | Scribe's formula, in short |
|---|---|---|
| 1827/1/18 | 1849–1854 | "Альтаристомъ/Викарнымъ онаго Кс. …", "окрещенъ младенецъ / окрещена дѣвица", "урожденной [fem. form] [family, gen. pl.]", time of day ("по утру", "вечеромъ"), no townships |
| 1827/1/22 | 1867–1874 | "мѣстнымъ Кс. …", "окрещено дитя", "урожденной [fem. form, often nominative] [family, gen. pl.]", "сего 1869 года", scribe often writes а for о ("гада", "са всѣми") |
| 1886–1890 (book 1, folder plunge-1886-1890) | 1886–1890 | "Викарнымъ Кс: …", "[father] и [mother] изъ [maiden, gen. pl.] [family, gen. pl.]", "[X]ской волости" |
| 1827/1/24 | 1896–1902 | "мѣстнымъ викарнымъ Кс. …", "окрещено дитя именемъ: …", "изъ [maiden, gen. pl.] [family, gen. pl.] зак. суп.", digits in the birth date, "того-же прихода" |
| 1827/1/25 | 1902–1907 | "Кс./Ксендзомъ …", "окрещенъ младенецъ", "урожд. …", patronymics ("Іосифа Станиславова"), "внѣбрачной … дѣвицы", "Подкидышъ" |

Every Lithuanian translation uses these renderings, so a term is the same in every entry
and can be corrected everywhere at once. Not reviewed by a native speaker. It follows common
Lithuanian usage for translated metrical books. The glossary was revised on 2026-11-11 after
the reviews of books 18, 22, 24 and 25. Changes go here first.

**Three principles behind every rule below.**

1. **Translate what is written; never restore what is not.** Do not reconstruct a
   "true" Lithuanian surname, place or feminine form. Where the glossary gives a mechanical
   rule, apply it even if you think you know the real name.
2. **One form per thing.** The same word, person, priest or place gets the same
   Lithuanian form in every entry of a book. Where a choice exists, this glossary makes it.
   Do not choose differently.
3. **Lithuanian alphabet only** in the sentence (a ą b c č d e ę ė f g h i į y j k l m n o p
   r s š t u ų ū v z ž, plus "ch"). Never use Cyrillic, ł, w, rz, sz, cz, ż, ó, ý, q or x, and never
   Latin "sh", "kh" or "ts". The **only** exception is the clergy names listed under Clergy,
   which keep their Polish spelling.

Notation in the examples: "→" means "write". [?] marks an uncertain reading or a name
reduced mechanically. Square brackets also mark words that are not in the source (see
Placeholders).

## 1. Formula

The three fields are always built in this order. Every element is required. If the source
lacks an element, write it in square brackets in its place (e.g. "[Romos katalikų]",
"[Sakramento]", "su [visomis] Sakramento apeigomis"). Only do this when the word is really
absent from a complete sentence. A word that is present but garbled ("таинстьа",
"Таиоства") is present.

**Baptism field.**
`[date] Plungės Romos katalikų parapijos bažnyčioje [priest phrase] su visomis Sakramento apeigomis pakrikštijo kūdikį, vardu [NAME].`

**Parents field (married parents).**
`[estate of the family, gen. pl.], teisėtų sutuoktinių [father, gen.] [family surname, gen.] ir [mother, gen.], gim. [maiden name], sūnus/duktė, gimęs/gimusi [date] [place], [parish].`

**Godparents field.**
`Krikštatėviai buvo [status] [godfather] ir [godmother][, mergina / , našlė / , [husband, gen.] žmona].`

## 2. Estates, status words and clergy ranks

### Estates and status of lay people

Estate words describe the whole family. They come first in the parents field, in the
genitive plural, and are followed by ", teisėtų sutuoktinių" (Template A). Status words
that belong to the man alone (soldier, official) go in the singular before the father's
name. "teisėtų sutuoktinių" then follows the mother's maiden name (Template B), even if the
scribe wrote the plural ("Отставныхъ Солдатовъ").

- Template A: "Valstiečių, teisėtų sutuoktinių Jono Beliavskio [?] ir Veronikos, gim. Talimovna [?], duktė, …"
- Template B: "Atsargos kareivio Juozapo Šliožkaus [?] ir Rozalijos, gim. Pstravičavna [?], teisėtų sutuoktinių duktė, …"
- Both apply when the source has both ("Крестьянъ … волости отставнаго солдата …"): "Plungės valsčiaus valstiečių, atitarnavusio kareivio Jono … ir Onos, gim. …, teisėtų sutuoktinių sūnus".

("of that/this church") are **not translated**: the church has just been named. Do not
write "šios bažnyčios" or "tos bažnyčios" for them.

## 3. Dates

- **Numerals always**, in every date of the sentence: "1888 metų balandžio 13 dieną". Never
  number words ("trisdešimt pirmą", "devyni šimtai šeštų"), never Roman numerals, never a
  mixture ("dvidešimt [septintą?]").
- **Fixed order: year, month, day.** Write "[year] metų [month, gen.] [day] dieną" even when
  the source writes "родившійся 7 Апрѣля 1904 года" or "Августа 5 дня 1849 года" →
  "gimęs 1904 metų balandžio 7 dieną". Always "metų" (never "metais", never "m." in the
  sentence; "1902 m. liepos 29 d." is only for notes). Always "dieną", including after a
  placeholder.
- "сего 1869 года" → "1869 metų" ("сего" is not translated). "1902 г." → "1902 metų".
  "числа" → "dieną".
- Months (gen.): Января sausio, Февраля vasario, Марта kovo, Апрѣля balandžio, Мая gegužės,
  Іюня birželio, Іюля liepos, Августа rugpjūčio, Сентября rugsėjo, Октября spalio, Ноября
  lapkričio, Декабря gruodžio. Misread month words must still be translated ("mada" = мая →
  gegužės; "Mara" = марта → kovo). A month heading in the margin ("Февраль", "Мѣсяцъ") is not
  part of the entry. Leave it out.
- Dates are never converted between the Julian and Gregorian calendars, and no calendar note
  is added.
- **Time of day** (mostly 1849–1854, after the birth date): по утру / поутру / утромъ → ryte;
  вечеромъ → vakare; около полудня / въ полдень → apie vidurdienį; ночью → naktį. It is
  placed after "dieną": "gimusi 1849 metų rugpjūčio 2 dieną ryte Jazdaičių [?] kaime, Plungės
  parapijos". Never "rytą".

### Placeholders (one vocabulary everywhere)

| Situation | Write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| one or more digits unreadable | [?] in place of the digits | "kovo 2[?] dieną", "kovo [?]5 dieną", "18[?]9 metų" |
| a number read but uncertain | number + [?] | "kovo 13 [?] dieną" |
| a whole slot (day, month, year, name, word) unreadable | [neįskaitoma] in that slot; "metų"/"dieną" kept | "1872 metų sausio [neįskaitoma] dieną"; "1868 metų [neįskaitoma] [neįskaitoma] dieną Pokiršnų [?] kaime" |
| the source does not give it | [nenurodyta] | "1851 metų rugpjūčio [nenurodyta] dieną" |
| the line or page breaks off | [tekstas nutrūksta] at the break | |
| a clear but impossible reading (birth after baptism, 1811 in an 1850 entry) | the number as read + [taip parašyta] | "gimęs 1811 [taip parašyta] metų …". Never "[sic]" or "[kaip parašyta]". If the reading itself is unsure, use [?] instead. |

Notes and inserted remarks use Lithuanian quotation marks „…“. Never leave Cyrillic in the
Lithuanian sentence; put a Cyrillic reading only in the notes field.

### 4.3 How to form a village or manor name (mechanical)

The name before "kaime / miestelyje / dvare / palivarke / valsčiaus" is a **genitive**.
Never a nominative ("Luknianai kaime"), never a Russian locative ("Luknianach kaime",
"Luknianuose"), never a name after the type word ("kaime Pokean"). Follow these steps:

1. If the place is in the fixed table (4.4 or 4.5), use that form, also for OCR variants of
   the same spelling.
2. If the source has a **plural locative** -ахъ / -яхъ (also misread -ах, -ак, -акъ, -ахь,
   -ян, -ach), remove it and add the Lithuanian genitive plural. This is the same for every
   Lithuanian plural declension, so it invents nothing:
   - after -ахъ → **-ų**: Кансудахъ → Kansudų [?]; Видейкахъ → Videikų [?];
     Мардосахъ → Mardosų [?]
   - after -яхъ → **-ių**: Пруселяхъ → Pruselių [?]; Шаркяхъ → Šarkių [?]
   - -цахъ / -цяхъ → **-čių** (Russian ц here renders Lithuanian č in -čiai village
     names): Яздайцяхъ → Jazdaičių [?]; Вайнайцяхъ → Vainaičių [?]; Милошайцяхъ →
     Milošaičių [?]
   - -чахъ / -чяхъ → -čių; -шахъ → -šų; -жахъ → -žų; -джахъ → -džių
   Then add [?].
3. A **plural nominative** (-ы / -и: "Мейяны", "Лукняны") is treated the same way:
   Mejanų [?] kaime, Luknianų [?] kaime.
4. A **singular** form (locative -ѣ / -е / -и, e.g. "въ деревнѣ Поушнѣ", or a nominative
   such as "фольваркъ Ольгополь"): you cannot know the Lithuanian declension. Remove the
   Russian ending and write the base + [?], undeclined: "Poušn [?] kaime",
   "Olgopol [?] palivarke".
5. Transliterate with the letter table in 7.1. Never keep ý, sh, w or Cyrillic.

Each priest has **one** fixed form per book, listed below. Use the listed form whenever the

gives only an initial ("А. Шаровскимъ"), write the listed given name in full.

spelling (ш sz, ч cz, ж ż, в w, -ичъ -icz, -скій -ski; or Lithuanian letters if a Lithuanian
ending is written), add [?], and report the name in the notes.

| -исъ / -иса / -исовъ | -is (gen. -io): Можейкисовъ → Možeikis |
| -ысъ / -ыса / -ысовъ | -ys (gen. -io) |
| -усъ / -уса / -усовъ | -us (gen. -aus): Шимкусовъ → Šimkus; Стонкуса → Stonkus |
| -юсъ / -іусъ / -юсовъ / -іусовъ | -ius (gen. -iaus): Поціусовъ → Pocius; Кубиліусовъ → Kubilius |
| -а / -ы / -ѣ of a man whose nominative ends in -а (Дрымба, Довгяла) | -a (gen. -os): "Antano Drymbos" |
| -о (Шилейко, Кунейко, Можейко, Лавринко) | -o, **unchanged in every case**: "Jeronimas Kuneiko", "Juozapo Lavrinko", "Igno Možeiko" |
| nominative singular ending in a consonant (Юндулъ, Видмонтъ, Савдаръ, Михайловъ) | the consonant-final form, **unchanged in every case**, no -as added: "Jonas Jundul", "Juozapo Vidmont" |
| genitive singular -а / -я after a consonant (Видмонта, Ступаля) | the consonant-final form: "Barbora, Antano Stupal žmona" |
| genitive singular -ова / -ева (Миклова, from Микловъ) | -ov / -ev, unchanged: "Antano Miklov [?] žmona" (a patronymic in -ова is handled by 7.5, not here) |
| **genitive plural -овъ / -евъ / -ёвъ / -іовъ / -ювъ / -ей after a stem without a Lithuanian ending** | **the stem without the Russian ending + [?], unchanged in every case** (see Step 3) |

**Step 2. Decline only the bases that end in -as, -is, -ys, -us, -ius, -a, or that came
from -skis, -ckis, -ičius.** Every other base (consonant, -o, -e, -i, -os …) is written
the same in every case. The father's surname is therefore genitive when it declines
("teisėtų sutuoktinių Jono Šimkaus ir …") and unchanged when it does not ("teisėtų
sutuoktinių Juozapo Stupel [?] ir …").

**Step 3. Genitive plurals whose base cannot be told.** A family name written -овъ / -евъ /
-іовъ / -ей after a stem that has no Lithuanian ending can hide -as, -is, -ys, -us, -a, -o or
-ė. Do not guess. Write the stem without the Russian ending, add [?], and never add a
Lithuanian ending. Never decline it as an -as noun. Never keep the -ov / -ev ("no
Daumovas", no "Stupelevo", no "Mickov"). Work only from the current entry. If the **same
entry** writes the same surname in a nominative singular with an ending (a godfather
"Осипъ Шакинисъ" for the family "Шакиневъ", or the margin), use that base instead.

**Further rules for men.**
- The family surname goes **with the father**: "teisėtų sutuoktinių Kazimiero Volovskio ir
  Petronėlės, gim. Abrutis, sūnus". It never goes after the mother or after "gim.".
- Endings -скій never become -ski or -skij. -ичъ never stays -ič.
- Husband or father named in a woman's description: the surname is that man's, in the
  Lithuanian genitive by the steps above: "съ Анною Игнатія Тимнскаго супругою" → "Ona, Igno
  Timinskio [?] žmona"; "съ Теодорою Виктора Стонкуса супругою" → "Teodora, Viktoro Stonkaus
  žmona". Never "-ago", "-ogo", "-aja", "Šintinei", "Zonikajos".

### 7.3 Women: surnames of godmothers, unmarried mothers and other women

**Nominative form (godmother, any woman in the nominative).** Bring the written feminine
form to the nominative, in Lithuanian letters, and keep its type:

| Written (any case) | Nominative |
|---|---|
| -ская / -ской / -скую / -скою (also misread -скаго, -skaja) | -ska: Янковскою → Jankovska; Бурневская → Burnevska; Мешковскою → Meškovska |
| -цкая / -цкою / -цкаго (misread) | -cka: Новицкою → Novicka |
| -овна / -овны / -овной / -овною / -евна / -евною / -ювна / -івна / -ювною | -ovna / -evna / -iovna: Криповною → Kripovna; Шакевною → Šakevna |
| -ова / -овой / -овою / -ева / -евою | -ova / -eva: Симаитовою → Simaitova [?]; Домаровая → Domarova [?] |
| any other -ая / -ою / -ей / -ею | -a (or -ė if the base ends in -ė/-е): -ая → -a, never -aja |
| a masculine-looking form actually written for the woman (Шакисъ, Стонкусъ, Бурмистровичъ) | as written, unchanged: Šakis, Stonkus, Burmistrovič [?] |

A woman's name that the draft gives with a Russian genitive -аго / -ого (book 22: "Аннаго
Пиворовнаго дѣвицею", "Ramokavnago", "Narvilovgo", "Novitskago", "Krivoševskago") is a
misread instrumental -ою. Treat it as such: "Ona Pivorovna [?], mergina", "Ramokavna [?]",
"Narvilovna [?]", "Novicka", "Krivoševska [?]". Exceptions: when the -скаго / -а / -ова
word follows a **man's** given name, or the phrase has супругою / женою / вдовою / дочерью
(even with the man's given name missing), it is that man's surname, in the Lithuanian
genitive (7.2): "Igno Timinskio žmona"; "Ona, Novickio žmona". Only a -аго form directly
on the woman's own name, with no husband or father in the phrase (typically before
"дѣвицею"), is the misread -ою.

Never make a woman's surname masculine (-skis, -ičius, "Gelumbickio", "Belskis" for
Бѣльскую). Never invent -aitė, -ytė, -iūtė or -ienė ("Narmontaitė" is wrong;
write the form written, e.g. Narmontovna [?]).

**Genitive (unmarried mother, any woman in a genitive phrase).** A woman's surname that
ends in **-a** declines like a Lithuanian -a noun. Every other ending stays unchanged. This is
the single mechanical rule; the reviewers disagreed, and this is the most mechanical
option.

| Nominative | Genitive | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -ska | -skos | valstietės Antaninos Čeblovskos [?] nesantuokinis sūnus |
| -cka | -ckos | |
| -ovna / -evna | -ovnos / -evnos | valstietės Petronėlės Krynovnos nesantuokinė duktė; valstietės Barboros Petrovičevnos |
| -ova | -ovos | |
| other -a | -os | valstietės Petronėlės [?] Liikos [?] |
| anything else (Stonkus, Burmistrovič) | unchanged | valstietės merginos Ievos Stonkus [?] nesantuokinė duktė |

The given name of a woman in a genitive phrase is always genitive too ("Marijos [?]
Gurnevskos", never "Pariia Gurnevskos").

### 7.4 Maiden names after "gim."

"gim." is always followed by the **nominative**, and only by the maiden name. There are two
cases, decided by how the register writes the maiden name:

**Case 1: written as a family genitive plural** ("изъ Мокусовъ", "изъ Якубовскихъ",
"урожд. Укринасовъ", "урожд. Шакевичей", 1886–1907): write the **nominative singular base**
by the men's procedure (7.2), including Step 3 for ambiguous stems.

| Source | Write |
|---|---|
| изъ Мокусовъ | gim. Mockus |
| изъ Якубовскихъ | gim. Jakubovskis |
| изъ Кубиліусовъ | gim. Kubilius |
| изъ Можейкисовъ (Книзелисовъ) | gim. Možeikis (and the family is Knizelis) |
| урожд. Шакевичей (Кучинскихъ) | gim. Šakevičius |
| изъ Касперовичей | gim. Kasperovičius |
| изъ Тарвидовъ | gim. Tarvid [?] |
| изъ Чесновскихъ | gim. Česnovskis |

**Case 2: written as a feminine form** ("урожденной Кедеравны / Касперовичевны / Орловской
/ Шимковна", 1849–1874 and 1902–1907): keep that feminine form, brought to the
nominative and written in Lithuanian letters (7.3 table).

| Source | Write |
|---|---|
| урожденной Кедеравны | gim. Kederavna |
| урожденной Касперовичевны | gim. Kasperovičevna |
| урожденной Орловской | gim. Orlovska |
| урожденной Пріялговской | gim. Prijalgovska |
| урожденной Шимковна (1867–1874 scribe writes the nominative) | gim. Šimkovna |
| урожденной Чесновской | gim. Česnovska |
| урожденной Шакисъ (masculine form written) | gim. Šakis |

Never write Russian -skaja ("gim. Beliavskaja"), never -ski, never an invented -aitė /
-ytė / -ienė, and never a masculine -skis for a form written -ская ("gim. Orlovskis" for
Орловской is wrong). The same family may therefore appear as "gim. Česnovskis" in one entry
(изъ Чесновскихъ) and "gim. Česnovska" in another (урожденной Чесновской). That is
correct: follow what each entry writes. This replaces the earlier wording "women's
surnames: keep the form written"; the two rules now agree.

**Parents' formula and where each name goes.**
- 1886–1907: "[father] и [mother] изъ/урожд. [maiden] [family] законныхъ супруговъ". The
  **first** surname is the mother's maiden name; the **second** is the family surname.
- 1849–1874: "[father] и [mother] урожденной [maiden, fem.] [family, gen. pl.]". Same
  order.
- Template: "[estate], teisėtų sutuoktinių [father, gen.] [family, gen.] ir [mother, gen.],
  gim. [maiden, nom.], sūnus/duktė, gimęs/gimusi …". Example: "Plungės valsčiaus valstiečių,
  teisėtų sutuoktinių Juozapo Knizelio ir Petronėlės, gim. Možeikis, duktė, gimusi 1896
  metų rugpjūčio 18 dieną …".
- Unmarried mother: no "gim.": "valstietės Onos Vilnovnos [?] nesantuokinis sūnus" (see
  Special cases).

### 7.5 Patronymics (1896–1907)

ONE construction: **given name, comma, father's given name (gen.) + "sūnus" / "duktė" in the
person's own case, comma, surname**.

| Case | Man | Woman |
|---|---|---|
| nominative | Juozapas, Stanislovo sūnus, Gričius | Ona, Antano duktė, Gekusovna [?] |
| genitive | Juozapo, Stanislovo sūnaus, Gričiaus | Onos, Antano dukters, Gekusovnos [?] |

- Source: "Крестьянъ Жоранской волости Іосифа Станиславова и Варвары урожденной Януневной
  Гричусовъ" → "Žarėnų [?] valsčiaus valstiečių, teisėtų sutuoktinių Juozapo, Stanislovo
  sūnaus, Gričiaus ir Barboros, gim. Janunevna [?], sūnus".
- The father's given name is taken from the patronymic stem and given its glossary form:
  Станиславовъ / Станиславова → Stanislovo; Іосифовъ → Juozapo; Антоновъ / Антоновна →
  Antano; Михайловъ → Mykolo; Казимировъ → Kazimiero; Игнатьевъ → Igno; Августиновъ →
  Augustino; Іоановна → Jono; Даниловъ → Danieliaus.
- Never "-ovo", "-ovas", "Juozapovo", "Antonovo", "Kazimirovo", "Michailovas", never
  parentheses ("Alberto (Danilo sūnaus) Borano") and never without commas.
- How to tell a patronymic from a surname: a word in -овъ / -ова / -овичъ / -овна / -евна
  is a patronymic **only if** its stem is a given name in the table **and** another
  surname follows it. If only one word follows the given name, it is the surname
  ("Варвары Петровичевны" → "Barboros Petrovičevnos").

## 8. Special cases

### 8.1 Twins

- Both twins in one entry ("окрещены близнецы", "близнята родившіеся"):
  - baptism: "pakrikštijo dvynius kūdikius, vardais Juozapas ir Kazimieras" (never
    "dvynukus", never "vardu A" twice);
  - parents: two boys "sūnūs (dvyniai), gimę …"; a boy and a girl "sūnus ir duktė
    (dvyniai), gimę …"; two girls "dukterys (dvynės), gimusios …". Always nominative
    (never "sūnaus ir dukters, gimusių"), never "vaikai (dvyniai)";
  - godparents, per child, in the order of the names: "Pirmojo kūdikio krikštatėviai buvo
    valstietis Martynas Otunovičius [?] ir [neįskaitoma], valstiečio žmona; antrojo kūdikio
    krikštatėviai buvo valstietis Vincentas Amim [?] ir Petronėlė, valstiečio Laurynto [?]
    Iundvia [?] žmona."
- One twin per entry ("сынъ изъ близнецевъ", "дочь изъ близнецевъ"): baptism as normal
  ("pakrikštijo kūdikį, vardu …"); parents "sūnus, vienas iš dvynių, gimęs …" / "duktė,
  viena iš dvynių, gimusi …". Never "sūnus iš dvynių", "(dvynių vienas)".

### 8.2 Illegitimate births

| Source | Lithuanian |
|---|---|
| Крестьянки Анны Видейковны незаконнорожденный сынъ | Valstietės Onos Videikovnos nesantuokinis sūnus, gimęs … |
| Крестьянки Плунгянской волости внѣбрачной Петронели Касперовичевной дочь | Plungės valsčiaus valstietės Petronėlės Kasperovičevnos nesantuokinė duktė, gimusi … |
| Крестьянки … внѣбрачной Стани славы Вилновной дѣвицы сынъ | Bernatov [?] valsčiaus valstietės merginos Stanislavos Vilnovnos [?] nesantuokinis sūnus |
| Крестьянки … Варвары Іміяновны Войчеховской дѣвицы дочь | … valstietės merginos Barboros, Imijano [?] dukters, Vojčechovskos nesantuokinė duktė |
| вдовы | valstietės našlės Onos Tomaševskos [?] duktė (if "незаконн-/внѣбрачн-" is written: nesantuokinė duktė) |
| незаконнорожденный сынъ, mother not named | nesantuokinis sūnus, gimęs … |

Rules: singular "valstietės" (never "valstiečių" for one mother). "merginos" (дѣвицы) and
"našlės" (вдовы) go **before** the given name, after the estate word. Everything about the
mother is genitive (given name, patronymic construction, surname by 7.3). "незаконнорожденный",
"внѣбрачный" and "незаконный" all → nesantuokinis / nesantuokinė.

### 8.3 Foundlings

"Подкидышъ неизвѣстныхъ родителей дочь, найденная 5 Августа 1904 года въ М. Плунгянахъ
того же прихода" → "Pamestinukė, nežinomų tėvų duktė, rasta 1904 metų rugpjūčio 5 dieną
Plungės miestelyje, tos pačios parapijos." Boy: "Pamestinukas, nežinomų tėvų sūnus, rastas
…". Only "pamestinukas / pamestinukė", never "rastinukas".

### 8.4 Emergency baptism ("съ воды") and supplied ceremonies

- "окрещенъ одною водою / съ воды / изъ воды" (baptised with water only, usually at home):
  "[date] … [person] pakrikštijo kūdikį, vardu X, tik vandeniu." If a person and place are
  given ("окрещенъ дома повивальною бабкою"), translate them as written.
- "дополнены обряды Крещенія / дополнено Крещеніе надъ [N]-лѣтнимъ дитятею по имени X"
  (rites supplied in church for a child already baptised with water): "[date] Plungės Romos
  katalikų parapijos bažnyčioje [priest] su visomis Sakramento apeigomis papildė krikštą
  [N] metų kūdikiui, vardu X." The age is written only if the source gives it. "su visomis
  Sakramento apeigomis" follows the general rule (brackets if absent): "su visomis
  [Sakramento apeigomis] papildė krikštą …".
- The printed 1849 form question ("одною водою или со всѣми обрядами окрещенъ?") is the
  form's heading, not an entry. See 8.7.

### 8.5 Entries from or about another parish

- Child born in another parish: the place and "[X] parapijos" as written:
  "… gimęs 1869 metų vasario 4 dieną Pokutovianų [?] kaime, Kulių parapijos."
- Baptism by another parish's priest: "[X] parapijos klebonas kunigas [name]" in the priest
  slot. His name follows the clergy rule for unlisted priests, + [?].
- Baptism performed elsewhere and copied into this book (another church named in the
  baptism sentence): write that church instead of the Plungė church, in the same pattern
  ("Kulių Romos katalikų parapijos bažnyčioje"). Keep the formula and its order.
- Parents from another parish or district are named with that unit before the estate:
  "Raseinių apskrities bajorų, …", "Telšių miesto miestiečių, …".

### 8.6 Rows with several entries

Some rows (one image line) hold two or more entries. In **each** field, number them "(1) …
(2) …" in the same order, and repeat the **full** formula in every entry. That includes the
full date, "Plungės Romos katalikų parapijos bažnyčioje", the priest and "su visomis
Sakramento apeigomis". Never write "toje pačioje bažnyčioje", "ten pat", "tas pats" for the
church, or headers such as "Trys įrašai.". Godparents: "(1) Krikštatėviai buvo … (2)
Krikštatėviai buvo …". If one entry of the row is a word-for-word copy of the previous one,
translate it as written and say so in the notes.

### 8.7 Printed blank forms and margin notes

A row that contains only the printed heading of the 1849 form ("Тысяча … Н. … сынъ или дочь,
родившійся или родившаяся … поутру, или около полудня, или вечеромъ") is not an entry.
Write "[Neužpildyto spausdinto blanko tekstas.]" in each field. Month headings, page numbers
and later margin remarks are not part of the entry; mention them only in the notes.

## 9. Checklist before returning (the ten most frequent errors in the reviews)

1. **Village name before "kaime" in the genitive** (4.3/4.4): "Luknianų [?] kaime", never
   "Luknianai kaime", "Luknianach kaime", "Luknianuose" or "kaime Lukniany". Same village,
   same form (table 4.4).
2. **"pakrikštijo kūdikį"** for every child; "vardu" for one child (even with two names);
   "dvynius kūdikius, vardais" only for twins.
3. **Dates in numerals, year–month–day, "metų" and "dieną"**: "gimęs 1904 metų balandžio 7
   dieną". Never number words, "m." or "metais" in the sentence.
4. **Formula complete**: "Plungės Romos katalikų parapijos bažnyčioje", "su visomis
   Sakramento apeigomis", in that order. Missing words go in brackets, and "toje pačioje
   bažnyčioje" is never used.
5. **Clergy**: exactly the form in the book's list (Szyłejko, Markiewicz, Mongird,
   Wojnowicz, Pacewicz, not Jacewicz in 1867–1874; Baltruken, Paškanis, Bajorinas,
   Bublis), "kunigas" written out, rank order as in section 2.
6. **Maiden name after "gim."**: nominative only. Case 1 base (gim. Mockus, gim.
   Jakubovskis) or Case 2 written feminine form (gim. Kasperovičevna, gim. Orlovska).
   Never -skaja, never -aitė, never -skis for -ская.
7. **No Russian endings on surnames**: no -ov/-ev/-ovo, -ago/-ogo, -aja, -ski/-skij, bare
   -ič. Ambiguous genitive plurals become stem + [?] (Stupel [?]). The father's surname is
   with the father, and declined only if its base ends in -as/-is/-ys/-us/-ius/-a.
8. **Women in genitive phrases**: the whole name is genitive ("valstietės Barboros
   Petrovičevnos", "Antaninos Čeblovskos"). Never a masculine genitive for a woman.
   Godmothers: -ska / -ovna, ", mergina" after the name.
9. **Given names from the table**: Jonas (Иванъ, Янъ), Vincentas (Викентій), Juozapas
   (Осипъ, Іосифъ), Pranciškus (Францъ), Ignas / Igno, Ona (Анна, Ганна), Ieva (Ева),
   Barbora, Dominykas, Domicelė, Petronėlė, Motiejus.
10. **Lithuanian alphabet only, and no untranslated Russian**: no Cyrillic, ł, w, rz, sh,
    kh, ts, ý. No leftover Russian words ("odnosemi", "mada", "zdginiago"). Patronymics as
    "Juozapo, Stanislovo sūnaus, Gričiaus". Status singular for a soldier father ("Atsargos
    kareivio …, teisėtų sutuoktinių duktė"). No "(valsčius nenurodytas)" before 1861.

## 10. Open questions for a native reader

Nothing here should be changed by translators. These are points a native speaker or a
historian should confirm. Changing any of them later would be a mechanical second pass.

1. **Undeclined surnames.** Consonant-final, -o and ambiguous-stem surnames are left
   undeclined in genitive positions ("teisėtų sutuoktinių Juozapo Stupel [?] ir …", "Jonas
   Jundul", "Igno Možeiko"). Is that acceptable, or should consonant-final forms be
   Lithuanianized with -as ("Jundulas", gen. "Jundulo"), as Lithuanian usually does with
   foreign names? The glossary chose not to add endings so as not to invent.
2. **Undeclined women's surnames** that do not end in -a ("valstietės Ievos Stonkus [?]"),
   and the genitive "-ovnos" of -ovna forms. Is "-ovnos" natural, or should -ovna stay
   undeclined?
3. **"Sakramento" with a capital S.** Two reviewers say standard Lithuanian writes
   "sakramento" in lowercase. It is kept capitalized because every existing entry has it.
4. **Comma before "vardu"** ("pakrikštijo kūdikį, vardu Ona"). One reviewer says appositive
   "vardu" normally takes no comma. It is kept for consistency with the existing entries.
5. **"mergina" vs "netekėjusi"** for дѣвица. One reviewer finds "mergina" colloquial in
   this register and suggests "netekėjusi", to pair with "nevedęs" and "našlė".
6. **Estate and military terms** chosen without native confirmation: "odnodvorcas"
   (однодворецъ), "laisvasis žmogus" (вольный человѣкъ), "piliečių" (Гражданъ, 1849–1854;
   perhaps "miestiečių" was meant), "atitarnavęs kareivis" (отставной), "neterminuotai
   atostogaujantis kareivis" (бессрочноотпускной), "kareivienė" (солдатка), "okolica"
   (околица) and "užusienis" (застѣнокъ), "dvaro patarėjas" (надворный совѣтникъ).
7. **Clergy rank order**: "dekanas kunigas X", "klebonas dekanas kunigas X", "vietinis
   kunigas vikaras X". Also whether a deacon written "Кс." should still be "diakonas"
   without "kunigas".
8. **Supplied ceremonies**: is "papildė krikštą [N] metų kūdikiui, vardu X" good
   Lithuanian? "kūdikis" for a child of several years is kept only because decision 5 fixes
   "kūdikis" for every child; "vaikas" may be better there.
9. **Twins' godparents**: "Pirmojo kūdikio krikštatėviai buvo …; antrojo kūdikio
   krikštatėviai buvo …". Is this natural?
10. **Patronymic construction** "Juozapo, Stanislovo sūnaus, Gričiaus": is it natural, or is
    "Juozapo Gričiaus, Stanislovo sūnaus" preferable?
11. **Village names** in 4.4 are mechanical genitives of the Russian spellings. Many are
    probably known villages (Покутовяны ≈ Pakutuvėnai, Ковшаны ≈ Kaušėnai, Пруселяй ≈
    Prūsaliai, Варкали ≈ Varkaliai, Труйки ≈ Truikiai, Мардосы ≈ Mardosai, Милошайцы ≈
    Milašaičiai, Жвирблайцы ≈ Žvirblaičiai, Нарвойши ≈ Narvaišiai, Леплавки ≈ Lieplaukė,
    Гондинга ≈ Gandinga). A later pass could replace the mechanical forms with gazetteer
    names. The -цяхъ → -čių rule (Jazdaičių) should also be confirmed.
12. **Township identifications.** Жоранская = Žarėnų and Корцянская = Kartenos are
    identified with [?]. Бернатовская (Bernatov [?]), Гинтеминская (Gintemin [?];
    possibly Gintališkė) and Лабардзевская (Labardzev [?]; possibly Labardžiai) are not
    identified.
13. **Clergy forms fixed by majority, not by documentation**: Baltruken (perhaps
    Baltrukėnas), Bajorinas, Kiršinas [?] (drafts read Кирминъ / Киртинъ; ш misread is
    assumed), Meritowicz [?] (book 1 altarist, read three ways), Kierpowski (vs Kerpowski),
    Kerwel (given names Jerzy and Leon both read: one priest or two?), Masilianis [?]. The
    decision to keep Lithuanian-letter forms (Paškanis, Laukšas) rather than Polish ones
    (Paszkanis) for priests whose Russian spelling has a Lithuanian ending should be
    confirmed.
14. **Given names**: Витольдъ → Vitoldas (or Vytautas?); Зофія / Софія → Zofija (or
    Sofija?); Марта → Morta; Домитилля → Domitilė; Тадеушъ → Tadas; Мадеушъ read as
    Тадеушъ → Tadas [?] (could also be Матеушъ → Motiejus); Розамія read as Розалія.
15. **"-осовъ" family names** (frequent in 1896–1902, e.g. Жимосовъ) are treated as an
    unrecognised ending (base "-os", undeclined), because "-ос" is often a misread "-юс" or
    "-ус". A reader may prefer a rule.
